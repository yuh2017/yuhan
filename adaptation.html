Disaster Resilience & Adaptation

Connecting Climate Risk, Human Behavior, and Adaptation Planning

Climate risk depends on more than the intensity of a natural hazard. It also reflects where people live and travel, the resources available to them, and how households and governments respond. My research examines these interactions between social and physical systems to understand how coastal flooding, sea-level rise, and compound hazards affect communities, and how planning decisions can reduce their impacts.

I combine agent-based modeling, empirical spatial analysis, and scenario assessment to connect individual behavior with community-level outcomes. My work spans household flood adaptation, government intervention, evacuation behavior, and land use change, with a focus on identifying actionable pathways for adaptation under climate and socioeconomic uncertainty.

Modeling Adaptation Decisions and Compound Flood Risk

My agent-based models examine how residents, local governments, and insurance interact in coastal flood adaptation. I use hazard projections, building and population data, and behavioral assumptions to compare adaptation decisions and their consequences under uncertain sea-level rise. Related studies assess building-level adaptation costs and benefits, simulate land use changes in response to compound flood risk, and examine evacuation activities and transport disruption during hurricanes.

Understanding Unequal Impacts and Policy Responses

My empirical research investigates the relationships between social vulnerability, disaster damage, public assistance, flood insurance, and population migration in the United States. These studies examine how risk and institutional support vary across places and communities, providing evidence for adaptation policies that account for differences in exposure, resources, and capacity to respond.

Supporting Planning under Climate Uncertainty

Across these studies, I use scenario analysis, risk assessment, and cost–benefit evaluation to examine adaptation options under uncertain sea-level rise and flood risk. My work compares building-level adaptation measures, household and government responses, and land use changes in coastal communities. By connecting modeled outcomes with evidence on social vulnerability, insurance, and disaster assistance, I aim to support adaptation planning that considers both long-term risk reduction and differences in communities’ capacity to adapt.
